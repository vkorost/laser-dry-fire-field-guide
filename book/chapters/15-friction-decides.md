# Chapter 15: Choosing a Setup You Will Keep Using

*Start from the surface you already own, then ask who owns the consumables, then ask what the arrangement looks like in three years. Compare features last, because the thing that decides whether you keep practising is how long it takes to start.*

## Friction decides

Chapter 12's finding is the one to carry into a purchase. Shooters stop when the setting up outlasts their appetite for the session, and the sharpest sign of it is the owners who kept the laser cartridge and abandoned the app that came with it.<sup>[1]</sup>

So the question is not which product does the most. It is which arrangement you will still bother to set up on a bad evening.

## Start from the surface, not the feature list

Chapter 4's groups are three answers to one question, and chapter 7 adds a fourth. The question is what the target is going to be.

**A printed target and a phone.** The commonest arrangement, the quickest to put away, and the only group whose working distances the vendors document, between about two feet and three yards depending on the product. If your room is small, this is the group that tells you in advance whether it fits.

**A target you supply, watched by software.** iDryfire's camera-sensor unit works with any paper target on a one-time licence. iShooter prints its own targets and scores up to 64 shooters locally. This group asks the least lock-in of you and the most setup.

**A screen or a projector.** Moving targets, drills that score themselves, a clock that starts itself. The cost is a target that emits light into the camera, and a screen surface that becomes equipment: anti-glare film for one product, frosted film for another, a translucent sheet for a third, so follow the instructions that came with yours.

**A target that scores itself.** No camera, and none of the problems that arrive with one. The LaserPET II, the G-Sight Electronic Mini Target and VPDOT's targets all state that no phone or app is needed. You give up the hit map and the history, and you get back every setup problem in this report.

That last option deserves more consideration than it usually gets. If what you need is repetitions with instant feedback, it delivers exactly that and asks nothing of you.

## Ask who owns the consumables

Whose paper, and whose cartridge. Two questions, and the answers do not travel together.

Strikeman opens the paper on its paid tier. LaserHIT opens it if you print its mark. G-Sight recommends its own cartridges. Mantis and LaserHIT both rule out infrared lasers, and iDryfire takes any paper target you care to print.

Price the paper and the cartridges for a year rather than for a session. A consumable available from one company is a cost you pay repeatedly, and it appears on no price list.

## Ask what the deal looks like in three years

Chapter 11's point is that the shape of the money outlives the amount.

Chapter 11 sets out the four shapes and what each one leaves you holding in three years. Two questions come out of it. What happens to your scores if the company stops, and which tier is the feature you actually want sitting in.

## Then match the goal

**For the fundamentals of the press**, the laser cannot help you. It lights at the break, so the hit describes the result rather than the press. A gun-mounted sensor measures the movement before, during and after the shot, and chapter 7 covers it.

**For competition timing**, measured time is what the software adds best, and chapter 10 is where the case for splits over totals is made.

**For simply continuing**, buy a step removed rather than a feature added. Chapter 12 names the two that owners credit most, and both of them work by deleting something that stands between you and the next repetition.

## Key takeaways

- Time to start decides whether you keep practising.
- Choose from the surface you already own, not from the feature list.
- Ask whose paper and whose cartridge before anything else.
- Judge the money by its shape rather than its amount.
- Match the instrument to the goal: sensor, splits, or a deleted step.
