# Chapter 7: Sensors, Reactive Targets and Laserless Trainers

*Three families sit next to the laser apps without being laser apps. A sensor on the gun measures the movement around the trigger press. A reactive target puts the electronics in the target and removes the camera entirely. A laserless trainer mounts a phone on the gun. They matter because each one answers something a laser and a camera cannot.*

## A sensor on the gun

**MantisX** is a rail-mounted sensor carrying a gyroscope and an accelerometer. It records movement before, during and after each shot, and scores the press rather than the hit.<sup>[1]</sup>

That is the whole point of it. The laser in a dry-fire cartridge lights when the trigger breaks, so by the time there is anything to photograph the press is over. A hit position tells you where the gun was pointing at the break. It cannot tell you what your hand did on the way there, and the movement on the way there is what you are trying to fix.

Since December 2025 the Mantis app can also show laser hits, on the vendor's own printed Smart Targets, and not on manual or custom targets.<sup>[1]</sup> Those targets are plain card with corner markers and no electronics, so this is camera work rather than a sensor in the paper.<sup>[2]</sup>

## A target that scores itself

Here the electronics are in the target and there is no camera, no phone and no app.

The **LaserPET II** from Laser Ammo is a standalone electronic target that needs no camera, phone or app.<sup>[3]</sup> The **G-Sight Electronic Mini Target** is self-contained in the same way.<sup>[4]</sup> **VPDOT** sells laser targets whose documentation states that no app or phone is required.<sup>[5]</sup>

What you give up is obvious: no hit map, no history, no splits beyond whatever the target itself displays, and a target face the size of the device rather than the size of a silhouette.

What you get back is everything the first six chapters have been spending pages on. No camera to frame. No calibration. No threshold. No room light to worry about, no reflection to be mistaken for a hit, no screen surface to film over, and nothing to update. You open a drawer and shoot.

A middle option exists. **Shooter Make Ready** uses wireless laser targets that send each hit to a phone app, so the detection happens in the target and the scorekeeping happens on the phone.<sup>[6]</sup> That buys back the history without buying back the camera.

## A phone on the gun

The laserless approach removes the cartridge instead of the camera.

**iMarksmanTV**, with iDryfirePath, clamps an iPhone to an unloaded pistol and connects it to a computer that shows the game on a television. There is no laser and no projector: the phone on the gun is doing the aiming.<sup>[7]</sup>

It is the inverse of everything else in this report. Instead of a camera watching where your light landed, a camera on the gun works out where the gun is pointed.

## The pistol that is not a pistol

The **SIRT** training pistol from Next Level Training is described by its maker as completely inert and unable to fire a bullet. It carries two lasers: a red one that shows trigger take-up and a green one for the shot itself, and the green laser stays on while the trigger is held.<sup>[8]</sup>

Both published instructors in this report name it, and they disagree about it, which chapter 1 covers. For the purposes of this chapter the interesting part is the take-up laser, because it is the one piece of laser hardware here that shows you something before the break.

## Choosing between them

Match the family to the fault you are trying to fix.

If your shots are going where you are pointing and you do not know why that is not where you wanted, the sensor measures the press. If you want repetitions with instant feedback and no setup, a reactive target gives you exactly that and nothing else. If you want recorded hit positions on a target face, you are back in the camera-based chapters and paying the setup tax that chapters 5 and 6 describe.

One caution worth carrying into any comparison. The improvement figures that appear in this category's marketing are not accompanied by published studies, so judge these products on what they measure rather than on what they claim it does for you.

## Key takeaways

- A gun-mounted sensor measures the press, which the laser is dark for.
- Reactive targets delete the camera, the calibration and the room light at once.
- Three of them state that no phone or app is needed.
- Wireless targets keep the history without bringing back the camera.
- The SIRT is inert, and its take-up laser shows the press itself.
- Judge all of them on what they measure rather than on what they promise you.
