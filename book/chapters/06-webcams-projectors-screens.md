# Chapter 6: Webcams, Projectors and Screens

*Putting the target on a screen buys you targets that move, drills that score themselves and a timer that starts itself. It costs you a target that emits its own light into the camera, and it makes the surface of your screen part of the equipment. Three vendors give three different instructions about that surface, and they cannot all be right for your room.*

## Four arrangements

**Dry Fire Online** draws targets in a web browser and watches the screen with a phone or tablet running its camera app. It does not support USB webcams and it does not support infrared lasers. The camera may sit left or right, above or below the screen, provided all four corners of the display stay in view.<sup>[1]</sup>

**Interactive Gun Range** is a free-to-play game on Steam, released on 29 July 2026, which uses a webcam to watch the display directly.<sup>[2]</sup>

**Laser Ammo's Smokeless Range** ships its own USB tracking camera with the software, and you supply the projector and a Windows computer.<sup>[3]</sup>

**iMarksman** is built around a projector, with a calibration sensor camera, an infrared filter and an infrared training handgun, and you provide the projector and screen.<sup>[4]</sup>

Those are four different answers to one question, which is how to get a camera to see a dot on a surface that is already glowing.

## The surface is equipment

This is the part people discover after buying. Take it first.

A projector screen reflects the dot back at the camera. A television or monitor emits its own light and reflects very little, which is why every screen-based product here has something to say about the surface, and why what they say differs.

**Interactive Gun Range** accepts a matte television, or a glossy one with an anti-glare film applied.<sup>[2]</sup>

**Dry Fire Online** says the opposite about anti-glare: anti-glare and polarizing filters on flat screens reduce the visibility of the laser dot, and it recommends a thin frosted film instead.<sup>[1]</sup>

**Laser Ammo** recommends a white projection screen, and for a flat-screen television it calls for a white translucent plastic sheet over the display.<sup>[3]</sup>

Three vendors, three surfaces: anti-glare film, frosted film, translucent white sheet. They are not describing the same optics, because a game watching an emissive display and a projector system watching reflected light have opposite problems. **Follow the documentation of the product you own**, and treat the category's general advice as noise.

## Why a glowing target is the hard case

What the camera has to do is pick out a small red mark on a surface that is already producing light of its own. A screen makes that harder in two ways at once.

The screen is lit, so the dot has less contrast to work with than it would on paper. And whatever the software draws is in the camera's view, so a reddish graphic is a candidate hit. That is why screen-based products are fussy about room light and about what colours they put on screen, and chapter 8 is the account of what it costs.

The practical consequence for a buyer is that a screen setup has more ways to go wrong and more adjustment available when it does. A phone on paper either sees the target or does not. A screen setup can see the target, see the dot, and still score a reflection off the bezel.

## What the screen buys you

Everything above is a cost, so be clear about the return.

Targets that move, which paper cannot do at all. Drills that reset themselves between strings. A clock that starts when the target appears rather than when you press something. Scenarios rather than a single target face. And on the projector systems, a target the size of a wall, which is the only arrangement in this report that gets close to the apparent size of a real range.

That is a genuine difference in what you can practise, not a cosmetic one. Chapter 14 is the method it has to fit inside.

## Key takeaways

- Four arrangements, all of them watching a surface that is already lit.
- The screen surface is equipment, not a setting.
- Three vendors ask for three different films, so follow the one that came with yours.
- Dry Fire Online rules out USB webcams and infrared lasers.
- A lit screen is the hardest detection case in this report.
- What you get back is movement, self-scoring drills and a self-starting clock.
