# Chapter 11: Free, Paid, Bundled

*How a product charges matters more than what it charges, because the shape of the deal decides what you still have in three years. There are four shapes here, and the costs that catch people out are the ones that are not prices at all: the paper you must keep buying and the cartridge that only works with one app.*

## The four shapes

**Free to play.** Interactive Gun Range is free to play on Steam. Its licence is worth reading before you build a routine on it: it grants a personal, revocable, non-exclusive, non-transferable and limited licence for non-commercial use, last updated in September 2025.<sup>[1]</sup> Revocable is the operative word, and it is ordinary for software of this kind.

**A free app with paid unlocks.** The commonest shape in the category. Mantis Laser Academy's app is free and sells holster draw, par-timed draw and reaction drills separately.<sup>[2]</sup> Strikeman's app is free with its own targets and sells an Open Target mode on a subscription.<sup>[3]</sup>

**A subscription.** Dry Fire Online's free tier allows two shots per round, with its paid levels raising that to five, thirty and fifty, and split times and hit scores sitting on the top tier.<sup>[4]</sup> iShooter's app listing offers in-app purchases while the vendor describes a basic subscription as required.<sup>[5]</sup>

**Bought outright, or bundled with hardware.** iDryfire sells its camera-sensor unit with a licence and states that there is no subscription.<sup>[6]</sup> Laser Ammo's Smokeless Range is a purchased package of camera and software with add-ons sold separately, and you supply the projector and the computer.<sup>[7]</sup>

## Two shots per round

That one number deserves its own line, because it is the clearest illustration in the category of what a free tier is for.

Two shots is not a trial of a drill. It is a demonstration that the detection works. You cannot shoot a string, you cannot measure a split, and you cannot run a par. What it proves is that the camera sees your laser, which is genuinely the thing most likely to stop you, so it is a sensible thing to prove first.

Just do not mistake that demonstration for the product itself.

## The costs that are not prices

Here is where the money actually goes once the app is paid for.

**The paper.** Several products in chapter 5 are built around the vendor's own printed targets. Strikeman's free app wants its own marked targets, and Mantis prints Smart Targets with corner markers the camera needs to see.<sup>[2, 3]</sup> You can print some of these at home and some you buy, and the ones you buy are a recurring cost that appears on no price list.

**The cartridge.** G-Sight's vendor recommends its own cartridges.<sup>[8]</sup> Mantis needs a red or green visible laser and will not work with infrared.<sup>[2]</sup> LaserHIT also rules out infrared.<sup>[9]</sup>

**The surface.** Chapter 6's film and screen requirements are equipment, and the sheet or film is a purchase even though no vendor sells it to you.

A consumable you can buy from only one company is a price you pay repeatedly. It is worth more attention than the difference between two subscription tiers.

## What to look at instead of the amount

Three questions, and none of them is about money.

**What happens if the company stops?** A one-time purchase that runs on your own machine keeps working, while a subscription with server-side scoring stops when the service does.

**What do you have to keep buying?** Paper, cartridges, film. Price those for a year, not for a session.

**Which tier has the feature you actually want?** Splits, draw time and hit scores sit behind paid tiers in two of the products here, so judging a product by its free version can tell you very little about the product.

## Key takeaways

- Four shapes, and the shape outlives the amount.
- A free tier can be a demonstration rather than a trial.
- The recurring costs are the paper, the cartridge and the screen film.
- A one-time purchase on your own machine survives the vendor.
- Check which tier holds the feature you want before judging a product by its free version.
