# Chapter 13: Coaching, and What a Hit Cannot Tell You

*A hit position shows where the gun was pointing when the trigger broke and nothing about what your hand did on the way there. That one boundary explains the whole shape of coaching in this category, including why the most useful of it comes from products that never look at the target at all.*

## What a hit cannot tell you

The laser lights at the break. Before that there is nothing to photograph, so no camera-based product can see the press that produced the shot.

**What a hit cannot tell you** is therefore most of what you want to know. A group low and left is a result, while the flinch, the squeeze of the whole hand and the dip of the muzzle as you anticipate the shot all happen in the half second before the light exists.

Camera products therefore describe the result while sensor products describe the press, and no amount of software turns the first into the second.

## Spoken calls, which are the most useful thing a camera can offer

LaserHIT's Virtual Spotter speaks the split time, the group, and the windage and elevation calls.<sup>[1]</sup>

That is worth more than it sounds, for a reason chapter 2 implies: you are downrange with a gun, looking at the target, not holding a phone. Anything that has to be read competes with the thing you are supposed to be doing, while a spoken call leaves your eyes where they belong.

Windage and elevation are also the right level of advice for a camera to give. They describe where the group sat, which is exactly what a hit position supports, and they stop short of telling you why.

Mantis is explicit about that boundary from the other side: its Laser Academy app does not do diagnostics, and the diagnosis lives in its sensor instead.<sup>[2]</sup>

## What the sensor says instead

The Mantis sensor measures the movement of the gun itself, before, during and after each shot, which is the arrangement chapter 7 describes in full.<sup>[2]</sup>

That is the window a camera cannot reach, and it is why the sensor products are the benchmark for coaching in this category even though they are not laser products.

What to do with it is a separate question. A movement trace tells you that the muzzle dipped. Whether it dipped because of your trigger finger, your grip or your expectation of recoil is a judgement, and it is yours or your instructor's rather than the instrument's.

## What to ask of any coaching feature

Three questions that sort the useful from the decorative.

**What does it measure?** A hit position or the movement of the gun, because everything a coaching feature can honestly tell you follows from that one answer.

**Does it tell you what happened or why?** A split time and a windage call are measurements, while a cause is an interpretation, and an interpretation that arrives with no measurement behind it is worth nothing.

**Can you check it?** Fire ten deliberate shots with a known fault, a heavy trigger finger or a deliberate flinch, and see whether the feature notices. If it does not, you have learned that cheaply.

## Key takeaways

- The laser lights at the break, so no camera can see the press.
- Spoken calls beat anything that makes you read a screen while shooting.
- Windage and elevation are the right level of advice for a camera to give.
- Mantis leaves diagnostics to its sensor rather than its laser app.
- A movement trace tells you the muzzle dipped and never why it dipped.
- Judge any coaching feature by what it measures before you judge what it tells you.
