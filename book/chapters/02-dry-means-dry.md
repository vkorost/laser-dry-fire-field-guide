# Chapter 2: The Rules Do Not Relax When the Gun Is Empty

*Laser dry-fire training happens with a real firearm in your hands, in a room in your house, with nobody watching. The safety rules apply exactly as they do on a range. The software has no part in any of this: nothing a camera does changes what is in the chamber.*

## Dry means dry

Stoeger puts the whole chapter in one line. The operative word in dry fire is *dry*.<sup>[1]</sup>

No live ammunition. Keep it out of the practice area entirely, not in a drawer in the same room. Cheng says the same thing with no exceptions attached, and adds the habit that makes it stick: check the magazines, then check them again.<sup>[2]</sup>

Put a backstop behind whatever you are aiming at. Stoeger's stated reason is worth reading twice: in case a live round somehow works its way into the mix. A basement is close to ideal, and without one, a solid wall is the next best thing.

That is the logic of someone who has thought about being wrong. The rule keeps ammunition out of the room. The backstop is for the day the rule fails.

## The rules, combined

Both books state the standard rules, and Cheng says directly that they continue to apply during dry fire.<sup>[2]</sup> Taken together, this is the working set:

- Treat every firearm as if it is loaded.
- Keep it pointed in a safe direction, and never let the muzzle cover anything you are not willing to destroy.
- Keep your finger off the trigger until you are ready to fire.
- Keep the firearm unloaded until it is ready for use.
- Be sure of your target and what is beyond it.

Cheng ranks the second one first, on the grounds that a shooter who forgets every other rule still injures nobody if the muzzle is pointed somewhere harmless.<sup>[3]</sup> That ordering is worth adopting in a room where the whole point is that the gun is empty, because it is the rule that does not depend on being right about the chamber.

## Why the checks are repeated rather than careful

Cheng tells the story of a SWAT commander doing routine dry fire at home, aiming at a phone book, using magazines from a bowl he was certain were empty. He pressed the trigger and the gun fired. The round went about two thirds of the way through the phone book.<sup>[3]</sup>

Nobody was hurt, and the man never worked out how a live round reached a magazine he was sure of.

The useful part is the shape of the failure. This was not obvious carelessness. It was an experienced shooter, in a routine he had done before, defeated by one magazine that was not what he believed it to be. Both books' arrangements are built for exactly that: ammunition out of the room so the magazine cannot be wrong, and a backstop for when it is anyway. Cheng's lesson is that you check the weapon repeatedly, and repeatedly is doing the work in that sentence.

## What the hardware does and does not change

A laser cartridge or barrel insert is fitted into your real firearm. Neither book treats that as a new hazard, and Cheng's description points the other way: a barrel insert goes into your real gun and will make the gun inert.<sup>[2]</sup> It is written as an assurance, in a passage about getting more hands-on time with your own pistol.

The software changes nothing here at all. No camera, app, projector or scoring routine in this report can see what is in your chamber or check your magazines, and none of them is built to. The responsibility sits with the shooter.

One correction before leaving the subject, because it is the kind of thing that propagates. Dummy rounds appear in Stoeger's equipment list, and their job there is to give the gun and magazines proper weight.<sup>[1]</sup> They are a weight device, not a safety device.

Everything in the rest of this report assumes the gun is empty.

## Key takeaways

- Live ammunition stays out of the room, and the backstop is for the day that fails.
- The safety rules apply during dry fire exactly as they do on a range.
- Muzzle direction is the rule that still protects you when you are wrong.
- Check magazines and chamber repeatedly rather than carefully.
- A barrel insert makes the gun inert, and dummy rounds are only for weight.
- No software in this report can tell you what is in your chamber.
