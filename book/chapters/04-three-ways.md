# Chapter 4: Three Ways to Build a Home Range

*Laser dry-fire products come in three shapes: a phone watching the vendor's printed target, camera software watching a target you supply, and a screen or a projector with something watching the display. What decides which suits you is the surface you already own and how much setup you will tolerate.*

## A phone watching printed paper

A printed target on the wall, a phone a yard or two in front of it, and an app that reads marks on the paper to work out where the target face is. Chapter 5 is this group in full.

**Mantis Laser Academy** watches a physical target with a phone or tablet on a tripod, and runs on both iOS and Android.<sup>[1]</sup>

**Strikeman** pairs its free app with its own marked targets, and sells a tier that opens it up to other targets.<sup>[2]</sup>

**G-Sight Laser Training Pro** puts a phone camera about a yard from an ordinary paper target, and the vendor recommends it only with its own cartridges.<sup>[3]</sup>

**iTarget Pro** holds the phone in a sled grip with its rear camera facing the vendor's target sheet, and its iOS app has been updated repeatedly since 2021.<sup>[4]</sup>

**LaserHIT** accepts a custom target only if that target carries the vendor's free registration mark, and it does not work with infrared lasers.<sup>[5]</sup>

Two questions separate these products, and they are separate questions: whose paper, and whose cartridge. Strikeman's paid tier opens the paper. LaserHIT opens it on condition you print its mark. G-Sight ties the cartridge instead. Ask both before you buy, because the answers do not travel together.

## Camera software watching a target you supply

Here the software is the product and the target is yours.

**iDryfire** sells a USB camera-sensor unit that connects to a Windows or macOS computer and works with any paper target, as a one-time licence with no subscription. Its separate Clip-N-Shoot kit clips a target over a phone's front camera instead.<sup>[6]</sup>

**iShooter** uses a camera to find laser hits on paper targets you print from the app, runs on Windows, macOS, Android, iOS and iPadOS, and its paid tier adds a local competition mode for up to 64 participants.<sup>[7]</sup>

This is the arrangement with the least lock-in and the most setup. Nothing is printed for you, nothing is framed for you, and the camera placement and the detection threshold are your problem. Chapter 8 sets out what that problem actually consists of, complaint by complaint.

## A screen or a projector

These are the products whose targets move.

Five products put the target on a display instead of on paper, and they differ in which display. **Dry Fire Online** uses a web browser. **Interactive Gun Range** uses a television or a projection. **Laser Ammo's Smokeless Range** and **iMarksman** are both built around a projector you supply yourself. **PlinkPal** keeps everything on a phone and an Apple Watch.<sup>[8]</sup>

What this group buys and what it costs are the same thing, which is a target that produces light of its own. Chapter 6 is both halves of that bargain, along with the surface each vendor asks you to put in front of the camera.

## The benchmark that is not a laser product

**MantisX** is a sensor clipped to the gun that measures the press itself rather than the hit, and chapter 7 is where it sits alongside everything above.<sup>[9]</sup>

It belongs here because it measures the part of the shot a laser cannot. The laser lights at the trigger break, so it says nothing about the press that produced it, and chapter 7 takes that further.

## The boundaries leak

A grouping earns its keep by telling you what to expect, and these edges are soft.

Strikeman's Pro tier moves it from the first group towards the second. Dry Fire Online uses a phone camera, which looks like the first group, and points it at a screen, which puts it in the third. iDryfire sells both a computer sensor and a phone clip-on, which lands it in two groups at once.

So read the groups as a description of the usual arrangement, and then check the specific product you are considering against the chapter that covers its group in detail.

## Key takeaways

- Three shapes: printed paper, a target you supply, a screen or a projector.
- Ask whose paper and whose cartridge, because the answers come separately.
- A screen buys moving targets and costs you detection margin.
- The reactive targets in chapter 7 remove the camera and the calibration entirely.
- Sensors on the gun measure the press itself, which is the one thing a laser can never show.
