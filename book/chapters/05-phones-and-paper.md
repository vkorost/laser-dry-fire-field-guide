# Chapter 5: Phones on Tripods, Paper on the Wall

*The commonest home setup is a phone on a tripod watching a printed target. It is the cheapest way in, the quickest to put away, and the one with the most rules about where you stand. Five products work this way, and they differ most in whose paper and whose cartridge you are tied to.*

## The arrangement

A printed target goes on a wall. Your phone goes on a tripod or in a supplied grip, rear camera facing the target. The app finds the target by marks printed on it, then watches for the dot.

Distance is the first thing to get right, and it is shorter than people expect.

Mantis Laser Academy asks for one to three yards between the phone and its printed target.<sup>[1]</sup> Strikeman asks you to keep the phone no more than about three feet away.<sup>[2]</sup> LaserHIT asks for about two to three feet.<sup>[3]</sup> You stand further back than the phone does, which is the point of the arrangement: the camera works at reading distance while you shoot from across the room.

## How the app knows where the target is

Two approaches, and the difference shows up every time you set up.

**Marks printed on the target.** Strikeman calibrates from four black and white squares at the target's corners.<sup>[2]</sup> Mantis uses corner markers on its printed Smart Targets, which are plain card with no electronics in them, and the camera has to see those markers.<sup>[1, 4]</sup> LaserHIT works the same way through its Reload Mark, and it will accept a target you print yourself provided that mark is on it.<sup>[3]</sup>

**Fitting the target into zones on screen.** G-Sight's grouping mode works from the bullseye and four corner dots, which you place inside zones marked on the phone display.<sup>[5]</sup>

iTarget Pro belongs to neither camp, because it looks for the target itself and there is nothing for you to line up.<sup>[6]</sup>

The practical difference is what happens when you move the phone. A marker-based setup re-aligns itself. A zone-fitting setup needs you to line it up again, which is seconds rather than minutes, but it is seconds every single time.

## What they report

All five give you hits on a target face and a time, and Mantis reports hit locations, split times and scores.<sup>[1]</sup>

What none of them publishes is how a hit touching a scoring line is resolved, which is chapter 9's subject and worth reading before you compare two products' scores.

## Whose paper, whose cartridge

These are two separate questions. They do not travel together.

Strikeman's free app works with its own marked targets, and its Pro subscription adds an Open Target mode for other targets.<sup>[2]</sup> LaserHIT will take a custom target carrying its free mark.<sup>[3]</sup> G-Sight's vendor recommends its own cartridges.<sup>[5]</sup> Mantis works with red and green visible lasers but not infrared.<sup>[1]</sup>

So one product opens the paper for money, one opens it for free if you print its mark, one ties the cartridge instead, and one tells you which colours of light it can see. Ask both questions about the specific product you are considering.

## Light, glass and mirrors

The failure mode here is a reflection the app scores as a hit.

LaserHIT puts it in its own setup guidance: keep mirrors, television screens and glossy surfaces out of the camera's view.<sup>[3]</sup> That is good advice for every product in this group, because the detector is looking for a red dot and a mirror will happily give it one.

Chapter 8 is the longer version of why, and the short version is that where the camera points matters more than any setting it has. Point it at the target and nothing else, and most of the problem is gone before you adjust anything.

## Key takeaways

- The phone sits close, from about two feet to three yards.
- Printed markers re-align themselves, and zone fitting needs you every session.
- Strikeman opens the paper for money, LaserHIT for a printed mark.
- G-Sight ties the cartridge, and Mantis and LaserHIT exclude infrared.
- Keep mirrors, screens and glossy surfaces out of the camera's view.
