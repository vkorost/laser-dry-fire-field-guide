# Sources

Every product statement in this book was checked against what the vendor itself
publishes. This file is that list, with the pages linked, so a reader can go
straight to the primary source instead of taking the book's word for it.

**How to read it.** A source here establishes what a vendor *claims*, and
nothing more. No product in this report was bought, borrowed or tested, so
"Laser Ammo states X" means exactly that — it does not mean X was observed to be
true. Where owners contradict a vendor, the book says so and attributes both.

**Every link below was fetched on 23 October 2026 and returned a page.** Nothing
here is a reconstructed or plausible-looking address. Where no page could be
found that supports a claim, the entry says so in place of a link rather than
pointing at something that merely looks right.

**Dates matter more than usual in this category.** Several of these products are
young and their documentation changes without notice. A page that has since been
edited is not an error in the book; it is the category moving, which is why the
book is dated on its cover.

## The owner-report survey

Chapters 8 and 12 draw on a survey of user reports gathered from app-store
reviews and shooting forums in October 2026. **It is cited as a body rather than
per-post, and deliberately carries no links.** Individual reviews are not durable
addresses, several are anonymous, and no single one carries any weight on its
own. What the survey supports is the *pattern* — the same complaint arriving
repeatedly, about different products, from people with no connection to each
other.

What it establishes: false hits from reflections, ceiling lights, shadows and
red indicator lights, reported by owners of Mantis, Strikeman, G-Sight and
iTarget; retuning sensitivity as the light changes; several false hits a second
under indoor lighting, and phantom bursts under certain compact fluorescent
bulbs; loose or unevenly seated cartridges, laser and sight offset, dimming
emitters and outright cartridge failures; two isolated reports of very low
detection rates, about 15 and about 20 per cent; target setup failing with hits
delayed or misplaced; hits registering well off target on a television; hours
spent calibrating a projector system, and a television working less well than a
projector; abandonment during setup; shooters who kept the cartridge and dropped
the app; and the value placed on visible and audible hit confirmation.

Where a single owner report stands alone, the book says "one report" rather than
reporting it as a finding.

## What this file does not cover

Chapters 15 and 16 add no product claim of their own; every product statement in
them is established in chapters 4 to 12 and attributed there.

Three statements re-checked at primary source on 23 October 2026 are recorded
above with the exact page they came from, because a secondary summary of one of
them had attributed a product to a vendor that appears nowhere in the vendor's
own text. The rule this file follows, and the book with it: **a quotation that
cannot be found on the vendor's own page does not go in**, however plausible it
sounds and whatever repeated it. The two Strikeman claims and the one Mantis
claim flagged above are held to the same standard, which is why they are flagged
rather than quietly linked to something adjacent.
