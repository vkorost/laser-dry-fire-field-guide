# Sources

Every product statement in this book was checked against what the vendor itself
publishes. This file is that list, gathered in one place so a reader can go
straight to the primary source instead of taking the book's word for it.

**How to read it.** A source here establishes what a vendor *claims*, and
nothing more. No product in this report was bought, borrowed or tested, so
"Laser Ammo states X" means exactly that — it does not mean X was observed to be
true. Where owners contradict a vendor, the book says so and attributes both.

**Dates matter more than usual in this category.** Several of these products are
young and their documentation changes without notice. Each entry carries the
month it was checked. A page that has since been edited is not an error in the
book; it is the category moving, which is why the book is dated on its cover.

## The owner-report survey

Chapters 8 and 12 draw on a survey of user reports gathered from app-store
reviews and shooting forums in October 2026. It is cited as a body rather than
per-post, deliberately: individual reviews are not durable links, several are
anonymous, and no single one of them carries any weight on its own. What the
survey supports is the *pattern* — the same complaint arriving repeatedly about
different products.

What it establishes: false hits from reflections, ceiling lights, shadows and
red indicator lights, reported by owners of Mantis, Strikeman, G-Sight and
iTarget; accounts of retuning sensitivity as the light changes; several false
hits a second under indoor lighting and phantom bursts under certain compact
fluorescent bulbs; loose or unevenly seated cartridges, laser and sight offset,
dimming emitters and outright cartridge failures; two isolated reports of very
low detection rates, about 15 and about 20 per cent; target setup failing with
hits delayed or misplaced; hits registering well off target on a television;
hours spent calibrating a projector system, and a television working less well
than a projector; abandonment during setup; shooters who kept the cartridge and
dropped the app; and the value placed on visible and audible hit confirmation.

Where a single owner report stands alone, the book says "one report" rather
than reporting it as a finding.

## Re-checked on 23 October 2026

A small number of statements were taken back to the primary source after the
first edition, because a secondary account of them turned out to be unreliable.
These are the ones where the exact wording matters, so they are recorded with
the page they came from:

- **Laser Ammo's television setup guide**, reached from the vendor's own
  question-and-answer page on whether a projector is required. It is the source
  for the filter material taped to the screen, the vinyl tablecloth, the
  explanation about the angle of reflection, and the unfixable case. A
  third-party summary of this guide attributed a specific theatrical diffusion
  gel to it; that product is not named anywhere in the vendor's own text, and
  the book does not repeat it.
- **Interactive Gun Range's FAQ**, for the answer about projectors and monitors
  quoted in chapter 6.
- **opendryfire's repository and README**, for the acoustic timing method in
  chapter 10.

The general lesson, which is also the book's editorial position: a quotation
that cannot be found on the vendor's own page does not go in, however plausible
it sounds and whatever repeated it.
